The Municipal Housing Safety Crisis

A serious fire in an old apartment building triggers public outrage. The government promises a Housing Safety and Renovation Programme. New rules may improve safety — but inspections, certificates and reporting requirements can also create administrative burdens.

Your challenge: improve housing safety without creating an unworkable system or shifting all compliance costs onto tenants, workers and small businesses.

Presenter role 1

Administrative Burden Auditor

Your role connects to Peeters (2020). You do not automatically oppose rules. You test whether organizational procedures create burdens, how they arise, and who carries them.

  • Challenge one procedure.
  • Ask whether it is necessary for the policy objective.
  • Identify whether the burden is formal or informal, intentional or unintended.
Presenter role 2

Media and Political Pressure

This role connects to Ringquist, Worsham & Eisner (2003). You change the salience of the issue and make political intervention more likely.

  • Announce a new media development.
  • Raise public pressure on government.
  • Ask whether technical complexity makes intervention harder.
Presenter debrief prompts

Peeters: Which actors produced administrative burdens? Were they deliberate or unintended? Which organizational practices reproduced them? Who bore the costs?

Ringquist et al.: Did public salience change political intervention? Did technical complexity constrain government? Did legislative direction alter the agency's work?

Course synthesis: How did political choices initiate rules, how did organizations multiply them, and how were the resulting burdens distributed?
